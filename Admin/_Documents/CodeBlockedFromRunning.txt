Smart App Control doesn’t offer a “developer mode,” so when it’s **On**, it enforces code‑integrity rules that block **unsigned, user‑built binaries** from running out of folders like `bin\Debug`. That’s exactly why your Visual Studio output DLL is being stopped.

The only way to keep SAC enabled while still running your own C# builds is to make your assemblies look like trusted code. You do that by **signing your DLLs with a certificate that Windows trusts**. Once signed, SAC treats them like legitimate, verified binaries and allows them to load normally.

Here’s the practical path developers use:

---

### ✔ Sign your C# assemblies with a trusted certificate  
Create a signing certificate, add it to Trusted Publishers, and sign your build output. SAC will allow the DLL because it’s now trusted code.

---

### ✔ Build in Release mode and sign the output  
Release builds are easier to sign and deploy. SAC doesn’t care about Debug vs Release; it cares about trust.

---

### ✔ Place your signed build in a trusted folder  
Program Files is always allowed. SAC respects that location.

---

### ✔ Keep SAC On  
Once your assemblies are signed and in a trusted path, SAC stays active and your Visual Studio projects run normally.
